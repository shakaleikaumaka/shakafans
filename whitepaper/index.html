1 · WHY — the problem with fan clubs today

Fan clubs today live on rented land. The platform owns the list, the platform sets the reach, the platform can evict the artist, and the fan's "membership" is a row in someone else's database. The artist never truly holds the relationship; the fan never truly holds proof they were there.

We are building the other thing: a fan club where the relationship itself is a public good — identity the fan owns, membership that is provable, privacy that is cryptographic rather than promised, and a ledger of generosity that anyone can audit and no one can fake. If the artist's website vanished tomorrow, the club would still exist — because it lives on the Ethereum layer 1, in contracts and records that belong to no company.

And because it is fully open source, it is not just Shaka's club. It is a protocol any artist on Earth can fork — the way the P.I.T. protocol lets any village keep its knowledge, this lets any musician, painter, or poet keep their people.

2 · THE PRINCIPLES — consent canon

Everything in this system obeys five laws, the same laws that govern the whole constellation:

1 · Consent is the default state. No fan is ever listed, named, or contacted without choosing it. The default is consent: pending.
2 · Every fan chooses their own light. 🌟 PUBLIC — celebrated by name on the fan wall — or 🔒 PRIVATE — sealed, never named, never listed, never leaked. Changeable anytime, no questions, only mahalo.
3 · The standing consent window. Consent is not a one-time click; it is a window that stays open. A fan can revoke — and revocation actually works, cryptographically, not just administratively.
4 · Fail closed. When in doubt, the system does the private thing. Ambiguity never leaks.
5 · Released with love or never. Data leaves the vault only by the fan's own hand, or not at all.

the test every feature must pass: would this still be honorable if a thousand artists forked it and a million fans joined? if not — it doesn't ship. ♾

3 · THE RECIPE — four ingredients + the settlement layer

Four open protocols, each doing the one thing it is best at, composed into something none of them is alone: a trust network between an artist and his fans.

INGREDIENT ① · PERSONHOOD

WORLD ID — one human, one voice, no doxxing

A fan club's first enemy is the bot flood: fake fans, scalped memberships, sybil noise drowning the real circle. World ID gives us proof of personhood — a fan proves they are a unique human without revealing which human. Verification happens once, with the orb; what the chain ever sees is a ZK nullifier — a proof that says "a real, unique human holds this key" and nothing else.

In the club: joining the circle as a verified human costs one proof. Trolling, farming, and impersonation become structurally expensive while real fans stay pseudonymous. The same primitive powers our consent-revocation flow elsewhere in the constellation (speakers in P.I.T. recordings revoking their own content via proof-of-humanity) — one rail, many gifts.

INGREDIENT ② · IDENTITY & CONSENT

ENS + EAS — the name the fan owns, the consent they anchor

A fan's identity in the club is not our database row — it is their ENS name, which they own outright. The family's own research (Track A, ranked 🥇) already chose ENS as the identity rail across the constellation; the Kingdom of Bhutan showed the pattern at national scale — anchoring, not storage: anchor the proof on mainnet, keep the data at the edges.

The light-choice rides the same rail as an EAS attestation (Ethereum Attestation Service — Track A 🥈, Track C): a fan signs shakafans: public from their own wallet, the consent registry anchors the attestation, and the wall shines. Revoking the light is a revocation attestation from their own key — instant, self-sovereign, no email to support, no admin queue. And the family's hardest law holds (Track C): never attest personal data on-chain — attestations carry choices and proofs, never identities; the revocation list stays derived data, regenerated, never hand-merged.

The artist's side is ENS too: gifts flow to shakalei.eth, a name, not a hex string. Subnames make the club legible — early members, founding fans, the first hundred — all issuable as subnames under the club's ENS, held by the fan, not by us.

INGREDIENT ③ · PRIVACY

ZERO-KNOWLEDGE — Semaphore: belong without being seen

The hardest promise in the club is the 🔒 PRIVATE fan: how do you give someone members-only access — video shares, the direct line — without ever learning who they are? The family's ZK track (Track B) ranked the architectures, and its silver medal is our gold fit: Semaphore — anonymous group membership where a fan proves "I am in the set of givers" (or "my tide is current") with a zero-knowledge proof, revealing nothing about which member they are. Its nullifiers give us the crown jewel of the consent canon: self-serve revocation — a fan can erase themselves with their own proof, no permission needed.

Behind the gate, the content rail follows Track B's champion: sealed-at-rest + wallet-auth — ciphertext at rest, keys released to whoever can prove membership. \$0 to run, and it fails closed: when in doubt, the bytes stay sealed. Public fans get the symmetric benefit: their wall name can carry a ZK attestation that it corresponds to a real gift — no fake flexing — while amounts stay theirs alone.

INGREDIENT ④ · DISCOVERY

THE GRAPH — the index anyone can query

Onchain data is true but hard to read. The Graph turns the club's events — gifts, memberships, light-choices, revocations — into a subgraph: one open, indexed, queryable view of the whole circle. The fan wall, the circularity ledger (gifts received → gifts given onward), the "149 doors kept open" counter — all of it renders from the subgraph, with no central API to rate-limit or shut down.

Because the index is open, anyone can build a better front-end for the club than ours — and the data layer remains honest, because it is the chain's own record, reorganized, not our version of it.

THE SETTLEMENT LAYER · ROOT OF TRUST

ETHEREUM L1 — where the promises live

The final home of every promise in this paper is the Ethereum layer 1: the membership registry contract, the gift ledger, the ENS names, the EAS consent attestations, the ZK verifiers. The pattern is proven at the highest stakes: the Kingdom of Bhutan anchored its national digital identity on Ethereum mainnet — anchoring, not storage; verification at the edges; covenant before code. What a kingdom does for citizens, a village can do for fans.

L1 is slow and expensive on purpose — it is the vault, not the dance floor. Day-to-day traffic (micro-tides, badges, wall updates) can ride an L2, but anchored to L1: the L2 is a convenience, the L1 is the truth. If every tool we built disappeared, an artist with a laptop and the contract address could reconstruct the entire club from L1 state alone. That is the standard.

5 · THE PATH — from today's warm rails to full onchain consensus

We refuse the false choice between "custodial but working" and "pure but imaginary." The club is alive today on warm rails, and each phase moves one more promise from our servers to the chain. Honesty about where we stand is part of the design:

PHASE 0 · NOW — THE WARM RAILS (live since Aug 2026)

Gifts by card through Stripe's hosted checkout (we never touch card data), the usher bot on Telegram verifies each drop against the pool, the consent-sealed registry holds the lights, and the fan wall publishes public names by the usher's hand. It works, it is warm, and it is honestly custodial: Stripe holds the money rail, Telegram holds the messaging rail, and we hold the registry. Every one of those is a promise Phase 1+ moves onchain.

PHASE 1 · THE ONCHAIN GIFT — the money rail opens

Crypto gifts to shakalei.eth become first-class members: the fan-club contract on L1 records gift → membership, and The Graph indexes it. The wall starts reading from chain + ENS records instead of our files. Stripe remains as an on-ramp for card-givers, but membership itself is born onchain either way.

PHASE 2 · SOVEREIGN IDENTITY — ENS names & self-serve light

Fans claim their place with their own ENS name; the light-choice becomes their own attestation, revocable by their own hand. Founding-member subnames for the early circle. The registry contract, not our database, is the source of truth for who is in.

PHASE 3 · PROVABLE HUMANS, PRIVATE FANS — World ID + ZK

World ID gating joins the circle: one human, one membership, sybil-resistant. ZK membership proofs give 🔒 PRIVATE fans full access with zero exposure — the usher never needs their email again. Consent states become ZK attestations; revocation is a proof, not a request.

PHASE 4 · FULL ONCHAIN CONSENSUS — the club that can't be switched off

Telegram and Stripe become optional mirrors, not pillars. Messaging moves to open, wallet-native rails; the money is pure L1/L2; the club's entire state reconstructs from L1. The artist and the fans hold the circle directly — consensus does the trusting, so nobody has to.
